# Deportation and Displacement Tracking

Track ancestors who were deported, displaced, or imprisoned during WWII and the Soviet occupation period.

## Autoresearch Configuration

**Goal**: For every ancestor suspected of being deported, displaced, or imprisoned (1939-1956), locate records documenting their experience and movements.

**Metric**: Number of deportation/displacement victims with documented records found

**Direction**: Maximize

**Verify**: Count ancestors tagged with `displaced: true` or `deported: true` who have at least one deportation-related source.

**Guard**:
- These are sensitive records about traumatic events. Handle with appropriate care.
- Names may be misspelled or transliterated differently in Soviet records.
- Some archives require formal requests and may take weeks to respond.
- Family oral history may be incomplete or inaccurate due to trauma.

**Iterations**: 6

**Protocol**:

1. **Identify potential deportees**: From family history, identify ancestors who may have been:
   - Deported to Siberia/Kazakhstan (1940-1941 Soviet deportations)
   - Sent to German forced labor (Ostarbeiter, 1942-1944)
   - Imprisoned in concentration camps
   - Displaced by border changes (1945 repatriations)
   - POWs in Soviet or German camps

2. **Categorize the deportation type**:

   **Soviet Deportations (1940-1941)**:
   - Four major waves: Feb 1940, Apr 1940, Jun 1940, Jun 1941
   - Destinations: Siberia, Kazakhstan, northern Russia
   - Targets: Polish officials, military families, "osadnicy"
   
   **German Forced Labor (Ostarbeiter)**:
   - Poles sent to work in German factories, farms
   - Marked with "P" badge
   - Records in Arolsen Archives
   
   **Concentration Camps**:
   - Auschwitz, Majdanek, and others
   - Arolsen Archives primary source
   
   **Post-War Displacement**:
   - "Repatriacja" from Kresy to Poland
   - Expulsion from former German territories
   - DP (Displaced Persons) camps in Germany

3. **Search Polish databases**:

   **Indeks Represjonowanych** (represjonowani.pl):
   - Index of Soviet repression victims
   - Search by surname
   - Links to archival sources
   
   **Ośrodek KARTA**:
   - Eastern Archive (Archiwum Wschodnie)
   - Testimonies and documents
   - Request access through website
   
   **IPN (Instytut Pamięci Narodowej)**:
   - Searchable victim databases
   - Request archival records formally
   
   **Muzeum II Wojny Światowej (Gdańsk)**:
   - WWII victim records
   - Oral history collections

4. **Search international databases**:

   **Arolsen Archives** (formerly ITS):
   - URL: arolsen-archives.org
   - Concentration camp records
   - Forced labor records
   - DP camp records
   - Free online search
   
   **Memorial (Russia)**:
   - Lists of GULAG prisoners
   - Deportation lists
   - Note: Organization declared "foreign agent" in Russia
   
   **Yad Vashem**:
   - For Jewish victims
   - Pages of Testimony

5. **Search by location of deportation**:
   
   If you know where ancestor was sent:
   - Siberia specific region: search Memorial database by location
   - Kazakhstan: Kazakh archives (limited online access)
   - German labor: Arolsen by factory/city location

6. **Amnesty and return records**:
   
   Poles released after 1941 (Anders Army):
   - Polish Institute and Sikorski Museum (London)
   - Records of Polish Army in USSR
   
   Post-war return:
   - PUR (Państwowy Urząd Repatriacyjny) records
   - State archives by destination region

7. **Create documentation**:
   For each deportee, create a detailed note:
   - Date and location of arrest/deportation
   - Transport details (if known)
   - Destination (camp, settlement, factory)
   - Date and circumstances of release/return
   - Sources for all information

8. **Update person files**:
   - Add `deported: true` or `displaced: true`
   - Add `deportation_date`, `deportation_destination`
   - Add `return_date` if applicable
   - Link all sources

9. **Log searches**: Record in Research_Log.md:
   - Databases searched
   - Request numbers (for formal archival requests)
   - Response timelines
   - Negative results

## Key Dates Reference

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Sep 1939 | Germany and USSR invade Poland |
| Feb 1940 | First Soviet deportation wave |
| Apr 1940 | Second wave (families of POWs, officials) |
| Jun 1940 | Third wave |
| Jun 1941 | Fourth wave (refugees from German zone) |
| Jun 1941 | Germany invades USSR |
| Aug 1941 | Sikorski-Maisky Agreement, amnesty for Poles |
| 1942-1945 | Poles as Ostarbeiter in Germany |
| 1944-1946 | Repatriation from Kresy |
| 1945-1950 | DP camps in Western Europe |

## Tips

- Family may have suppressed deportation history due to trauma or political reasons (PRL era)
- Multiple family members may have been deported together: search for siblings, parents
- Anders Army veterans have extensive documentation in London archives
- Arolsen Archives often has photographs with documents
