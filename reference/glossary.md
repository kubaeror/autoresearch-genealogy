# Polish Genealogical Glossary

A comprehensive glossary of terms encountered in Polish genealogical research. Polish records span multiple empires and languages due to the partitions (1795 to 1918), so researchers must navigate documents in Polish, Latin, Russian, and German.

## Languages Covered

- **Polish**: Native language, used throughout all periods
- **Latin**: Catholic church records, especially pre-1800 and in Austrian partition
- **Russian**: Civil and church records in the Russian partition (Congress Poland), especially post-1868
- **German**: Records in Prussian and Austrian partitions, civil registration in Prussian areas

### Calendar Note

**Julian vs. Gregorian Calendar**: The Russian partition used the Julian calendar (Old Style) until 1918. The Julian calendar was 12 days behind the Gregorian calendar in the 19th century and 13 days behind in the 20th century. When you see dates in Russian partition records, they are typically Julian dates. To convert to Gregorian (New Style), add 12 days for dates 1800 to 1899, or 13 days for dates 1900 to 1918. Some records show both dates (e.g., "15/27 January" means January 15 Old Style = January 27 New Style).

### Surname Notes

- **Przydomek**: A hereditary family nickname distinguishing families with the same surname. Example: "Jan Prusak Kowalski" = Jan of the Prusak branch of the Kowalski family.
- **Feminine forms**: Polish surnames often have feminine endings. Kowalski (m) becomes Kowalska (f). This convention appears in records.
- **Name spelling variations**: Expect significant spelling variations across records due to phonetic spelling, different languages, and clerical errors.
